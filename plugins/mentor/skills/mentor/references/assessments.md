# Assessments

## Wrap-up options

| Option | Time | What happens | Saved | Recommend for |
|--------|------|--------------|-------|---------------|
| **A. Quick recap** | 5–10 min | 2–3 recall questions in the chat | results | Sessions under 30 min, continuations |
| **B. Written** | ~15 min | One of: blurting essay, Feynman explanation, comprehension test | the essay or test + results | 30–60 min sessions |
| **C. Full** | 25–30 min | Blurting essay, then a Feynman explanation or comprehension test | both + results | Over 60 min, milestones |
| **D. No assessment** | 0 min | Just update the plan | session notes only | Exploratory sessions, short reviews |

Recommend one option based on how long the session ran, but always let the student choose.

## Techniques

**Blurting essay.** The student writes everything they remember about today's concepts, without looking anything up. Then you compare it with what was covered: list what they got right, what they missed, and any misconceptions. Save as `blurting-essay.md` (workspace mode), with the student's text verbatim followed by your evaluation.

**Feynman explanation.** The student explains one concept as if teaching a complete beginner, in simple words and with an example of their own. This is a teaching exercise, not a quiz. Probe the weak spots with "why?" and "what would happen if…?" until the gaps are clear. Save as `feynman-test.md`.

**Comprehension test.** 5–7 questions mixing free-text and multiple choice, from recall up to application ("which would you use here and why?"). Ask them one at a time and give feedback after each. Save as `comprehension-test.md` with questions, answers and feedback.

**Quick recap.** 2–3 short questions on the most important ideas. Feedback after each.

## Scoring

- Score each question or key idea, and report `X/Y` plus a percentage.
- For essays and Feynman explanations, list the key ideas the student should have covered and count how many they got right and accurate.
- Always name strong areas and areas to review. These feed `results.json` and the next session's review.
- Praise good reasoning even when the answer is wrong; be specific about what to fix.
